Order lifecycle and priority bids

Orders are good till cancelled (GTC). The order struct has no expiry, and nothing closes an order on a timer: it rests until it is fully filled or the maker cancels it. A signed message (swapWithSig, updatePriceWithSig, cancelWithSig) carries a deadline, but that only limits when the signature can be submitted.

See the protocol overview for the contracts and the call sequences for each step as calls.

A closed order is deleted. Its remaining bid settles to the FeeVault and any unused input returns to the maker.

Lifecycle

Book matching ranks the best opposing price first; a higher escrowed USDC bid breaks ties at one price, then earlier submission. Filler fills, flash fills and pool-fill retries target one order and do not follow the book's queue.

Loading diagram...

Notes:

  • updatePrice only re-ranks the order. It needs no unlock and triggers no matching; a later crossing order, matchBook, a filler or a pool-fill retry executes it. Each reprice increments orderReprices, which voids older signed reprices.
  • Router.matchBook accepts 1 to 8 pairs and does not retry pool liquidity. matchBook skips a pool while a new order on it is still being funded.
  • A crossing arrival on a pool with no active LP liquidity is staged in transient storage by IncomingMatching while it is funded, matched in memory against the book, and only its remainder is stored.
  • Book-match proceeds and refunds below the pool minimum are paid as PoolManager claims, so a token that refuses a recipient cannot block matching. Filler and pool fills pay ERC-20 output directly; native output is paid as claims (id 0). Flash fills pay the filler in claims.
  • Holders redeem claims through TokenClaims, NativeClaims or Router.withdrawClaims. Router.cancelAndWithdraw cancels and withdraws native input in one step.

Sources: Router, AsyncSwap, IncomingMatching, AsyncMatching, AsyncFiller.

Priority bids

Bids are optional and paid in USDC, so only numeraire pools accept them. A zero-bid order pays nothing and keeps time priority among zero-bid orders. A nonzero bid is escrowed and non-refundable; it must be at least min(0.10 USDC, 0.1% of the order's USDC notional). Bids rank same-price orders in the book; they do not buy block inclusion. The chain's priority fee is a different thing: the Router taxes it (the MEV tax, see the overview).

Loading diagram...

Bids always go to the FeeVault, never to LPs, so liquidity added just before a settlement earns none of them. A failed pool-fill self-call rolls back its fee and bid changes and keeps earlier book matches.

Raising a sell's minimum output requires a nonzero remaining bid to meet the new notional's bid floor. A partially filled order keeps its prorated bid when repriced to unchanged or lower terms. Sources: _reduceAfterMatch and _settleMatchedPriorityFee, settlePriorityFee, fill and cancel.

Top-ups, cancels and withdrawals

  • Router.topUpPriorityFee(order, addedFee) funds a larger bid. The order joins the back of its new fee tier; its input, limit price and maker age stay the same. See priority fee top-ups.
  • Router.cancelAndWithdraw(order) cancels and sends native input straight to the maker's wallet, given an exact claim allowance for id 0. ERC-20 cancels already return tokens.
  • Router.withdrawClaims(currencies, amounts) redeems up to 16 currencies of the caller's own claims.
  • Router.multicall(calls) batches any of these atomically, each as the caller.
