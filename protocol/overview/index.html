Async Swap protocol map

Async Swap is a Uniswap v4 hook that turns exact-input swaps into resting limit orders. This page describes the whole protocol in diagrams: which contracts exist, where funds sit, how an order is created, matched, filled, repriced and cancelled, and who is allowed to do what. Every box names a contract, library or function in src/, so the diagrams can be checked against the code.

1. Components

Loading diagram...

Anyone may open a pool on the hook, as long as it contains the chain's USDC (numeraire) and uses the dynamic-fee flag (beforeInitialize). Every order must meet two floors, both fixed when the pool opens:

  • Minimum order, on the USDC leg (a buy's input, a sell's minimum output). The hook's constructor takes it in whole dollars and scales it by the numeraire's decimals(). No check reads the pool price, so the initial price an opener chooses cannot block orders.
  • Asset floor, on the asset leg (a sell's input, a buy's minimum output): at least 0.0001 of a whole token, 10^(decimals - assetPrecision) base units with the deployed precision of 4. The hook reads the asset's decimals() once when the pool opens (native ETH counts as 18; a token without decimals() gets a 1-unit floor). An asset address with no code is rejected, so nobody can open a pool before its token exists and pin that floor at 1 unit. This blocks literal dust such as 1-wei sells; it limits quantity, not value.

Neither floor, nor the $0.10 priority-bid cap (10^(decimals - 1) of the numeraire), has a setter: the owner cannot change the rules under live orders. beforeAddLiquidity is a pass-through: anyone may add ordinary v4 range positions, and they are the inventory for the LP-fill path. No swap ever reaches the AMM curve directly, because beforeSwap consumes the whole input as an order; the only AMM swaps are the ones the hook itself performs when it fills a resting order from pool liquidity. The standalone MatchingEngine.sol is a benchmark harness and is not deployed.

FeeVault owns claims recorded in PoolManager. Crediting those claims does not call FeeVault.

2. Where funds live

Loading diagram...

Fees, by path. A book match and a filler fill (Router.fillOrder) take a 0.02% trade fee, rounded up, from what each side receives, and credit it to the FeeVault as claims: in a match the buyer receives the asset less 0.02% and the seller the quote less 0.02%; in a filler fill the filler pays exactly the maker's live minimum, the maker receives that minimum less 0.02%, and the filler receives the input less 0.02%. A maker's guarantee is therefore its minimum output minus 0.02%. Refunds and cancels are not charged. An LP fill sends 0.01% of the input to the FeeVault and swaps the rest at a 0.02% dynamic LP fee for the active v4 positions; it still pays the full minimum. A pool fill that stops at the best book level (see section 4) pays the 0.01% only on the input it swapped and must meet the maker's minimum pro rata. calculatePoolFee (a surcharge on top of the minimum) and calculateHookFee are zero.

Dynamic fee ordering (DynamicFeeOrdering). The first pool fill in a window of 2 blocks records the pool's tick before it as the reference. A later pool fill in that window that moves the price back toward the reference pays a dynamic fee from its output: half the price gap it closes, amountOut * (gapBefore + gapAfter) / 4 in units of 0.01% (one tick), capped at half the output. The gap is averaged over the fill's start and end ticks, so splitting a backrun into several orders costs about the same. Half of the dynamic fee is donated to in-range LPs and half is credited to the FeeVault as claims (all of it to the FeeVault if no liquidity is in range). The dynamic fee is taken in the fill's output currency; the maker's minimum must still be met after it. A fill that crosses the reference pays only on the share of its output from before the reference. Fills that move the price further from the reference, and fills after the window, pay none. See dynamic-fee-ordering.md.

Fee events. Every fee the hook moves is logged by the hook, with its pool, so indexers never have to recompute fees from gross amounts:

AsyncOrdersMatched and AsyncOrderFilled carry gross amounts; AsyncOrderLiquidityFilled carries the maker's output after the dynamic fee. Optional priority bids are escrowed as hook claims by addOrder and settle to the FeeVault: the filled share as input fills, the rest when the order closes. A zero bid incurs no escrow or transfer. Bids never go to LPs, so liquidity added just before a settlement earns none of them.

MEV tax ("Priority Is All You Need", Robinson & White). The Router charges every order creation and fill (swap, swapWithSig, fillOrder, fillFromPoolLiquidity) mevTax(): the transaction's priority fee per gas (tx.gasprice - block.basefee) times MEV_TAX_GAS, fixed at deployment and set per chain (see section 6). It is paid in ETH in msg.value, on top of any native deposit, and credited to the FeeVault as native claims. On a chain that orders transactions by priority fee, searchers racing for the same position bid that fee up, and the tax turns most of the bid into protocol revenue instead of sequencer revenue: with MEV_TAX_GAS about 30 times an order's gas, roughly 97% of it. A transaction with no priority fee pays nothing, so wallets should send orders with a zero priority fee. Cancels, claims and matchBook are not taxed, so a maker racing to protect an order pays only gas. The tax only captures value when searchers compete; it does not by itself protect the order they race against.

The tax covers order creation and fills through the Router only. A searcher can still race untaxed: it reprices one of its own resting orders with updatePrice and settles it with matchBook, or pool-fills it through tryFillOrderFromPoolLiquidity with its own contract as executor. No user loses funds this way, since every fill still respects the resting order's limit; the protocol only collects less. This is acknowledged (the 2026-11-01 Pashov review, findings 4, 5, 6 and 9).

An order-to-order match pays both sides, and returns any remainder left below the pool minimum, as PoolManager claims rather than token transfers, so a recipient a token refuses cannot block matching. Holders redeem ERC-20 claims through TokenClaims and native claims through NativeClaims, to any recipient, after approving the redeemer for that claim id, or to their own wallet through Router.withdrawClaims with exact per-claim allowances. Filler fills and LP fills pay the maker's ERC-20 output directly. Minting bid claims creates hook debt immediately; the PoolManager unlock must settle that debt, including for custom executors. The Router pays it with settleFor(hook). All pools rank equal prices by remaining bid, then sequence, so zero-bid orders remain time-prioritized. There is no FIFO mode or mode switch.

The accounting invariant is fuzzed by the matching invariant suites: the hook's claim balance for each currency equals the sum of remaining inputs of open orders plus escrowed bids, and nothing is created or destroyed by a match. The hook's own token balance is zero between transactions, so sweep can only move stranded ETH or tokens sent to the hook by mistake, never escrow.

3. Placing an order

Loading diagram...

Signed orders follow the same path through swapWithSig. The maker signs one Permit2 PermitWitnessTransferFrom whose witness is OrderSignatures.hashOrder(order), so the signature both moves the input and commits to the order. A relayer can also attach a signed SetExecutor grant for the maker's first order on a pool. A supplied grant is always applied and its nonce spent, so it cannot be replayed to re-authorize the Router after the maker revokes it.

4. Everything after placement

Filler fills, book continuation, LP retries, repricing, cancellation, pool setup, native-claim redemption and fee collection are drawn step by step in contract-call-sequences.md; the lifecycle and priority-bid flowcharts are in order-lifecycle.md. Three facts to keep in mind while reading them:

  • Book matches and filler fills take 0.02% of what each side receives, credited to the FeeVault as ERC-6909 claims inside the PoolManager; the LP-fill path charges 0.02% to active v4 positions and 0.01% to the FeeVault, plus a dynamic fee on fills that move the price back within 2 blocks of a pool fill (half to LPs, half to the FeeVault). Matching compares the signed limits, so the fee never changes which orders cross. calculatePoolFee and calculateHookFee are zero.
  • A new order goes to the better venue first. If the pool has active liquidity and its spot price is strictly better than the best opposing book level, the pool goes first, limited at that level: it fills the order up to the level and the book matches the rest (a fill that stops there pays out as claims, and a remainder below the pool minimum that the book leaves is refunded). Otherwise the hook matches the book first and the pool must take all of any remainder. The comparison uses spot price, so it ignores the pool fill's fixed fees and price impact, but inside the dynamic-fee window it counts a fill back toward the reference as half its gap (in ticks) worse, the fee it would pay. The limit is the level's tick, one tick higher for a swap that lowers the tick, so the pool never fills past a resting order's exact price, and it stays inside the range v4 accepts, so an order at an extreme price cannot turn pool fills off. A pool fill that would leave the pool with no active liquidity reverts, so the order uses the book and the pool is never left where no liquidity is active.
  • updatePrice only re-ranks the order in the book. It needs no PoolManager unlock and triggers no matching; a later crossing order, matchBook, a filler or an LP retry has to execute it.
  • matchNewOrder is gated by a transient slot that beforeSwap writes for the current transaction. executeOrder, cancelOrder and tryFillOrderFromPoolLiquidity check isExecutor[maker][caller] instead.

An order's identity is keccak(chainId, poolId, owner, zeroForOne, salt). The hook assigns the salt at creation and emits it in AsyncOrderCreated; later calls recompute the id from the maker's struct, so a maker needs the event or an indexer to cancel or reprice.

5. Authorization

Loading diagram...

Router.multicall runs several Router calls in one atomic transaction, each as the caller, so a maker can cancel many orders and withdraw claims at once without granting any other contract executor or operator rights. It is not payable: native-value actions (swap with native input, fillOrder with native output) must be sent on their own, so no two calls can count one msg.value. For the same reason a taxed action inside multicall needs a transaction with no priority fee.

Revoking the Router with setExecutor(poolId, router, false) stops Router fills of the maker's orders on that pool until the maker grants it again; book matching can still consume them. It never traps them: the hook lets its own Router cancel without a grant, because the Router cancels only for order.owner (cancelOrder, cancelAndWithdraw) or with the owner's signature (cancelWithSig), and a cancel only returns input to the owner. Any other executor still needs the maker's grant.

Signed maker actions use unordered nonces: AsyncSwap.nonceUsed for SetExecutor, UpdatePrice and CancelOrder (the Router burns cancel nonces through AsyncSwap.useNonce, so invalidateNonce revokes any of them), and Permit2's own nonces for order witnesses. The Permit2 witness binds maker, both tokens, amounts, bid and pool; it leaves out sqrtPrice (never read, the hook consumes the whole swap) and salt (assigned by the hook).

6. Deployment shape

Loading diagram...

script/deploy.sh <chain-key> [dry-run|broadcast|verify] runs this sequence against script/config/chains.json. It passes DEPLOY_MIN_ORDER_USD (default 10) and an asset precision of 4; the constructor reads the numeraire's decimals (1 to 20) and derives the base-unit minimum and the $0.10 bid cap itself. The Router's MEV_TAX_GAS comes from the chain's deployment.mevTaxGas, next to deployment.ordering, how that chain orders transactions. It is 20,000,000 on Unichain, whose blocks are ordered by priority fee, and 0 on chains ordered by arrival (Robinhood Chain), by block builders (Ethereum, Sepolia) or not yet confirmed (Arc, MegaETH), where it would only tax ordinary tips. deploy.sh refuses a chain with no value and a nonzero value on a chain not ordered by priority fee. verify reads the constructor arguments back from the deployment transactions. On chains whose gas token has an ERC-20 twin (Arc: native USDC and 0x3600…0000 are one balance) never initialize a native-currency pool; pair the ERC-20 USDC with a normal ERC-20 asset.

See also price-key-crossing.md for how truncated price keys decide whether two orders cross, and mainnet readiness for the release gate.

Signed cancellation typed data includes uint64 createdSequence after orderId; read it through orderCreatedSequence(poolId, orderId). It does not include the order's size: a cancel covers whatever remains, so a partial match before it lands cannot void it. Signed repricing includes the order's size when signed, uint64 createdSequence and uint32 reprices (orderReprices(poolId, orderId)), and the relayer passes that size to updatePriceWithSig. The size and new minimum are a price: if matches have shrunk the order, the hook applies it to the remainder, rounding the minimum up. Only reprices move reprices, so an unrelayed reprice signature cannot undo a later reprice, and replacing an identically identified order does not revive old signatures of either kind. See repricing-signatures.md. invalidateNonce revokes the shared grant, reprice, and cancel nonce space.
