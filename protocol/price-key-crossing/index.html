Price keys and crossing

Price keys

Every resting order is indexed on its pool's book by a uint128 price key computed in AsyncFiller._priceKey (TickPrice.key): the Uniswap v4 tick of the order's exact limit.

  1. Take the order's currency1/currency0 ratio: amountOutMin / amountIn when it sells currency0, amountIn / amountOutMin when it sells currency1.
  2. Take its floor tick: the largest tick t (spacing 1, price 1.0001^t) priced at or below the ratio (TickPrice.floorTick, via TickMath.getTickAtSqrtPrice).
  3. Order ticks by numeraire per asset: t - MIN_TICK + 1 when the numeraire is currency1, MAX_TICK - t + 1 when it is currency0.

Keys run from 1 to 2 * MAX_TICK + 1. Ratios outside the v4 price range (about 2^-128 to 2^128) share the end keys. Each level is 0.01% wide at any price and for any token decimals, so two orders sharing a nonclamped key are within approximately 0.01% of each other. The end levels do not have this spread bound. The exact limit stays on the order: keys order the radix tree; they do not by themselves decide whether two orders trade.

Crossing rule

AsyncMatching._crosses(buyKey, sellKey, buy, sell) decides whether a buy and a sell can trade:

  • buyKey > sellKey is a cross and buyKey < sellKey is not. The key is monotone in the exact price, so a strict ordering of keys always reflects a strict ordering of exact prices.
  • Equal keys are decided at full precision:
cross  <=>  buyIn / buyOut >= sellOut / sellIn
       <=>  buyIn * sellIn >= sellOut * buyOut
       <=>  floor(buyIn * sellIn / sellOut) >= buyOut      (sellOut and buyOut are nonzero)

implemented as Math.mulDiv(buy.amountIn, sell.amountIn, sell.amountOutMin) >= buy.amountOutMin. Orders cannot be created or updated with a zero minimum, so the divisor is never zero. Both escrowed inputs are bounded by signed 128-bit settlement amounts, so their product and its quotient by a positive minimum fit in 256 bits, including at clamped end levels.

An exact tie (buyIn * sellIn == sellOut * buyOut) counts as a cross and executes.

Consequences

  • Equal-key noncrossing heads no longer terminate selection when another pair is eligible. Each priority subtree stores its minimum exact ask or maximum exact bid. The search chooses the higher-priority child if its summary admits a crossing order, otherwise the other child. Each lookup traverses at most 128 branches without scanning or persistent cursors.
  • Automatic arrivals and resting-book matching use the same selection rule: the exact highest bid and exact lowest ask in their best levels. The subtree extremum is the eligibility threshold, so only orders at that exact extremum qualify; remaining fee and sequence break exact-price ties. If these extrema do not cross, no pair at the boundary crosses. Prices grouped in the same level never let a worse exact price jump ahead by paying more.
  • Every genuine cross, including the tie, produces a non-zero match in _matchAmounts that pays each maker at least their signed minimum for the input consumed. This was checked exhaustively for small amounts and on randomized large near-tie pairs.
  • Matching compares the signed minimums, not the post-fee proceeds, so the 0.02% trade fee (taken from each side's proceeds afterwards, rounded up) never changes which orders cross.

Example of equal keys without a cross: a sell asking 1000.05 and a buy offering 1000.02 USDC per asset sit on the same tick, but the buy is below the ask, so the pair rests.

Level width and the price-priority bound

Within one tick, priority is bid then sequence, not exact price, so a higher-bid order can trade ahead of one priced up to 0.01% better. Across ticks, price always comes first. Under the earlier WAD-ratio key a level was 10^(assetDecimals - usdcDecimals - 18) USDC/asset wide, which is about 10% of the price for an 18-decimal asset at $0.00001, and the clamped keys 1 and type(uint128).max merged arbitrarily distant prices (audit 2026-10-23, finding 1). Ticks remove both: the width is relative and fixed, and only prices outside the v4 range share the end keys.

Historical counterexample and repaired regression

test/security/CoarsePriceBlocking.t.sol creates a pool with an 18-decimal asset and 6-decimal USDC fixture at 1,000 USDC/asset, minimum order 1 USDC, and no active LP liquidity. A sell offering 2e15 - 1 asset base units for 2_000_000 USDC base units has the same key as a 1,000 USDC/asset order, but a slightly higher exact ask. Its 100_001-unit bid outranks the 100_000-unit bids of 20 honest sells. Under the previous head-only policy, twenty matching buys stopped at that blocker, and 100 retries made no progress. The Python legacy_matching=True mode preserves that baseline.

With exact eligibility, the same twenty buys execute against the honest sells without cancelling or outbidding the blocker. The blocker remains open, preserving its escrow and bid. The updated CoarsePriceBlockingTest asserts that outcome through the real Router/hook/PoolManager, and tests the mirrored incoming-sell case. Generated EligiblePythonVectors.t.sol exercises normal prices, both clamps, blockers on both sides, mutations, partial fills, balances and fee claims.

With tick keys the blocker above is one level worse than the honest asks, so it cannot outrank them at all. A level is 0.01% of the price wide. That bounds price separation, not delay or backlog. The repaired selector guarantees finding a crossing pair in a valid book when one exists; successful settlement and transaction submission remain required. It does not guarantee that every low-priority order eventually fills under continuing higher-priority arrivals.

The companion model is in the local uv4 checkout under src/uv4/priority_protocol/, with results and explicit modeling boundaries in docs/priority_protocol/. PriorityPythonVectors.t.sol checks 100 independently generated Python matching cases against the real matcher; PriorityPythonHashes.t.sol checks order identities and signature struct hashes.

Maintenance, gas, and verification boundaries

EligiblePrice.sol compares full 512-bit cross products and stores two-word exact bounds at priority-tree nodes. Append/remove repair the affected ancestor path. Every partial fill refreshes the bound even if neither the tick key nor the remaining fee changes. New storage is appended to each price level; existing live trees would need their summaries initialized before using this code. These local changes have not been deployed.

The protocol-controlled index work is O(128 M) for M matches. Eight is the existing per-call cap, not a per-transaction cap. Token code and external settlement costs are outside this structural bound. EligibleGas.t.sol measures composed batches through a real unlock; no numerical worst-case gas certificate or universally optimal batch size is claimed.

The updated whitepaper is in the local asyncswap/whitepaper checkout. Its new section contains integer safety and search arguments, gas curves, and the constrained parameter objective.
